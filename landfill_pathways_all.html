Air pathway: landfill gas carries pollutants far beyond the fenceline

As organic waste decomposes it generates landfill gas — primarily methane and CO₂, but also a complex mix of toxic volatile organic compounds (VOCs). These gases migrate through soil and can enter nearby homes through cracks, utility entry points, and floor drains — a process called soil vapor intrusion.

Landfills are supposed to collect and flare this gas, but standard flares don't destroy everything — VOCs and certain PFAS compounds survive combustion. Older or smaller sites may have no gas controls at all, and a landfill can produce gas for more than 50 years after closing.

Health effects: Respiratory disease, asthma aggravation, cancer (bladder, leukemia, lung), neurological damage, cardiovascular disease, eye and throat irritation.

Pollutants released

Benzene Toluene Vinyl chloride Ethylbenzene Xylene Hydrogen sulfide Ammonia Methane FTOHs (volatile PFAS)

Water pathway: leachate carries dissolved chemicals into groundwater and waterways

As rainwater and moisture percolate through buried waste, they dissolve chemicals and carry them downward as leachate. Most landfills truck or pipe it to municipal wastewater treatment plants not designed to remove these chemicals — PFAS passes through intact.

Leachate also leaks into groundwater through aging or compromised liners, contaminating private wells that may never be tested. In 2024, 36 New York treatment plants processed over 1 million gallons of PFAS-laden leachate every day.

Health effects: Cancer, kidney and liver damage, reproductive harm, low birth weight, neurological damage, immune disruption.

Pollutants in leachate

PFOA & PFOS Lead Arsenic Mercury Cadmium Benzene Trichloroethylene Pesticide residues Ammonia
