Modern Landfill: methane from the same area, again and again

Aircraft and satellites detected methane plumes starting from the same part of the landfill in 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025.

Methane plumes: Carbon Mapper, source CH4_6A_1000m_-78.97427_43.21048. Aug. 15, 2021 and Oct. 5, 2022: Global Airborne Observatory aircraft (plumes GAO20210815t135403p0000-A and -B; GAO20221005t151743p0000-A). July 28, 2024: NASA’s EMIT instrument on the International Space Station (emi20240728t191154p13038-A). May 9, 2025: Planet’s Tanager satellite (tan20250509t164310c00s4001-A). Emission rates are Carbon Mapper’s estimates for the moment of each pass; no rate was estimated for May 2025. Wind from the HRRR weather model where reported.

EPA inspection: U.S. EPA Region 2 letter to Modern Landfill, Inc. (Sept. 2, 2025): EPA’s Aug. 7–8, 2024 inspection found 19 leaks at 94 points; Modern reported three surface exceedances for the third quarter of 2024. The letter describes alleged violations, offered for settlement.

Plume colours are adapted from Carbon Mapper’s plume images (light = less methane, dark red = more). A plume shows methane in the air at the moment of a pass, spreading downwind from where it starts. No detection is shown for 2023, which likely reflects the absence of a pass rather than of emissions.

Base map: Esri World Imagery; image dates vary. Exhibit: Full Circle Future.
