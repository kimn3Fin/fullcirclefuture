Why does this detection matter?
Large methane plumes can indicate significant landfill emissions and may help identify problems such as gas collection system failures, cover issues, or other leaks. Many of these emissions sources can be investigated and repaired.
However, in most states, landfill operators are not required to investigate or respond when large methane plumes are detected by satellites or aircraft. Starting in 2027, California and Colorado will require landfill operators to follow up on certain large remotely detected methane emissions.
Finding the emissions is the first step toward fixing them.

Why does methane matter?
Methane is an extremely potent greenhouse gas — about 80 times more powerful than carbon dioxide in the short term. Large methane releases from landfills can also signal the presence of other harmful pollutants, including benzene, hydrogen sulfide, and PFAS. The good news is that proven, cost-effective solutions exist to dramatically reduce these emissions.
